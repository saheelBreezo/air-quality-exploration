# Urban Air Quality Assessment and Sensor Network Optimization in The Hague

## Background

Air pollution poses significant risks to human health and the environment, contributing to respiratory and cardiovascular diseases and premature mortality. Fine particulate matter (PM₂.₅) is a major pollutant of concern because its small particle size allows it to penetrate deep into the respiratory system. PM₂.₅ can be emitted directly from sources such as road transport, residential heating, and industrial activities, or formed through atmospheric reactions involving precursor gases, including sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.

Although air quality across Europe has improved over recent decades, air pollution remains an important environmental health concern. In the Netherlands, air quality is monitored through a nationwide network of official monitoring stations operated by organizations such as the National Institute for Public Health and the Environment (RIVM), GGD Amsterdam, and DCMR Rotterdam. These stations provide continuous, quality-controlled measurements, while community-operated, low-cost sensors contribute additional observations through initiatives such as the *Samen Meten* data portal. Although community sensors can improve spatial coverage, differences in calibration, sensor accuracy, and environmental conditions may affect the reliability of their measurements.

This project assesses urban air quality in **The Hague, Netherlands**, using observations from official monitoring stations and community-based air quality sensors.

The analysis focuses on evaluating the spatial distribution and reliability of available measurements, identifying gaps in the existing monitoring network, and proposing an optimized sensor placement strategy.

Particular attention is given to **schools and their surrounding areas**, where improved monitoring coverage could support a better understanding of local air pollution exposure.

The project addresses three main objectives:

1. **Air quality assessment:** Analyse spatial and temporal variations in air quality using official monitoring stations and community sensor observations.
2. **Sensor reliability assessment:** Compare community sensor measurements with reference monitoring stations to evaluate their consistency and reliability.
3. **Monitoring network optimization:** Identify suitable locations for additional sensors to improve spatial coverage, prioritizing areas surrounding schools.
